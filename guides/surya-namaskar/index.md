Surya Namaskar, the sun salutation, is a short sequence you can return to every day. Twelve poses. One breath for each. Then you repeat the cycle.

It is a complete warm-up on its own, and a steady way to begin a longer practice.

## What Surya Namaskar is

You start standing, palms together. The arms rise, the body folds, one leg steps back, and you pass through plank, the eight-point salute, cobra, and downward-facing dog before you stand again.

Each pose has a breath: inhale, exhale, or a short hold. One cycle is the whole wave. Five cycles is a short morning. Ten is a proper warm-up. One hundred and eight is a traditional full count, one for each bead of a mala.

The value is in the repetition. The spine, hips, and shoulders wake up. Breath and movement stay together. You finish knowing you did the thing you came to do.

## Why practice it with Bakasana

Counting poses in your head pulls you out of the pose. In a group, the people at the back also need to see which cycle they are on.

Bakasana keeps that for you.

The pose name, the breath, and a picture stay on the screen. Each pose lasts a set number of seconds, so the pace does not drift. The current cycle is large enough to read from across the room. You choose 5, 10, 20, 50, or 108 cycles before you start.

When the session is over and you want it to count toward the habit of returning, log it in the Bakasana app. The timer gets you through the salutations. The app keeps the streak, the history, and the reason to come back tomorrow.
